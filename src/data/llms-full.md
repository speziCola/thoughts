# Christopher Kuhrt

Christopher Kuhrt (also known professionally as Chris Kuhrt) is a design leader, product strategist, and design-systems advocate based in Freiburg im Breisgau, Germany. With more than ten years of experience across financial software, e-commerce, marketplaces, and digital platforms, he has moved from hands-on UX/UI and product design into cross-product UX strategy, design leadership, and organizational design. He is Head of Research & Design at Etops, where he is the functional and disciplinary manager of a team of designers and works to connect design, product, and engineering across complex financial products. He initiated the Abundantia design system, has helped organizations introduce Figma and improve design workflows, and co-founded UX Freiburg and the German UPA regional group in Freiburg. His professional focus is the systems behind strong products: problem framing, research, shared design foundations, collaboration, governance, and sustainable delivery.

## How I work

Christopher approaches design as both a product discipline and an organizational practice. He argues that improving the screens alone is insufficient if teams cannot agree on problems, decisions, responsibilities, and standards. His role is often to establish the conditions that let product teams make better decisions and deliver more coherent experiences.

**Strategic approach:**
1. **Establish foundations:** Clarify the UX vision, principles, governance, ownership, and existing knowledge; build trust and define what success should mean.
2. **Align direction:** Connect business goals, customer needs, and product priorities through shared discovery and evidence-based problem framing.
3. **Structure for scale:** Build design-system foundations, workflows, reusable patterns, and effective cooperation between design and engineering.
4. **Deliver, measure, evolve:** Collaborate through implementation, learn from research and feedback, coach teams, and improve standards continuously.

**Operational approach:**
1. **Understand context:** Identify stakeholders, goals, constraints, and what is already known.
2. **Discover and define:** Research user needs, synthesize evidence, and clarify the opportunity and criteria for success.
3. **Explore and validate:** Compare solutions, use workshops and prototypes, challenge assumptions, and validate at an appropriate fidelity.
4. **Deliver and iterate:** Support engineering, evaluate real use, and refine the experience after release.

**Leadership and collaboration:** He holds **direct disciplinary and functional responsibility** for the designers reporting to him. His role combines people management, individual development, feedback, professional guidance, and design direction with cross-functional leadership. He supports a participatory approach in which product managers, designers, and engineers can all challenge the problem definition and proposed solution. He uses visual workshops and shared artifacts to make assumptions explicit, emphasizes transparent decision-making, and values clear ownership without excessive silos. He advocates inclusive retrospectives, team-health practices, and feedback loops that lead to action.

**What matters:** Research quality, real user needs, accessibility, interaction consistency, clarity of decisions, maintainability, and measurable outcomes when they become available. His approach values honest distinctions between an idea, a prototype, an implemented feature, and a demonstrated result.

## Opinions and topics I talk about

- **Design is a systems problem as well as a craft discipline.** Product quality depends on decisions, structures, incentives, governance, and collaboration—not only polished screens.
- **UX should be an organizational capability.** Designers should help teams improve how they identify and solve problems, rather than become an isolated production function.
- **Outcomes matter more than output.** Completing prototypes, components, or tasks is not the same as demonstrating customer or business value; measured impact should not be claimed before evidence exists.
- **AI makes production faster but does not replace product understanding.** A convincing AI prototype can create premature confidence; it should remain an instrument for discussion, validation, and learning.
- **AI may shift attention back to research and problem discovery.** Automating parts of implementation can create more room for workshops, human interaction, and identifying worthwhile problems, provided teams use that opportunity deliberately.
- **Product discovery is a shared responsibility.** Designers, product managers, and developers can all challenge assumptions about what problem to solve, why it matters, and how best to approach it.
- **Design systems must go beyond visual libraries.** Accessibility, semantic tokens, interaction rules, component states, documentation, governance, and engineering integration determine whether a system is useful.
- **Behavior can be treated as a shared contract.** He is interested in separating design-system behavior and accessibility semantics from framework-specific rendering so shared design intent can be expressed more consistently.
- **Standardization should enable, not suffocate, teams.** He has written about centralized, decentralized, and hybrid design systems and the tradeoff between cohesion and autonomy.
- **Research should improve decisions.** Qualitative and quantitative evidence can reduce uncertainty and help avoid committing resources to insufficiently understood solutions.
- **Meetings should produce shared understanding.** Visual sessions, facilitation, mapping, and explicit follow-ups can be more useful than passive discussion and separate notes.
- **Culture and branding are connected to execution.** Renaming or restyling a company is not a substitute for aligning strategy, team behavior, product experience, and delivery.
- **Team health and inclusion influence collaboration.** Retrospectives, transparent feedback, and regular reflection help teams identify obstacles and act on them.
- **Shipping begins another learning cycle.** Design work should be reviewed against real user needs and available evidence after implementation.
